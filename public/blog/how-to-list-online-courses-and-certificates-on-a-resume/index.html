How to List Online Courses and Certificates on a Resume (With Examples)

Online learning can strengthen a resume when it is presented as evidence, not as a pile of course names. This guide shows where to place completed courses, verified certificates, projects, licences, in-progress study and expired credentials without overstating what they mean.

Quick rule: name the learning, state its status, add the provider or issuing body only when it helps verification, and connect it to a skill or project you can discuss. A course is not automatically a certification, and a certificate is not automatically a professional licence.

Start by identifying what you actually earned

Before adding anything to your resume, check the wording on the record you received. “Completed a course,” “earned a certificate,” “passed an examination,” and “holds a licence” describe different things. Use the most precise description you can support.

ItemWhat it showsHow to label it
Completed courseYou finished a defined learning experience. It may not involve an assessment or external validation.Course or Professional Development
Verified certificate or credentialAn issuer confirms completion, assessment, or a defined achievement under its own rules.Certifications or Professional Development
Professional licenceA regulator or professional body authorizes you to practise within a jurisdiction.Licences or Certifications, with jurisdiction and status
In-progress courseYou are studying but have not completed the requirements.Professional Development, marked “in progress”
Expired certificationA credential was once active but is not current.Usually omit; if relevant, mark “expired” and include dates

Where should online courses go on a resume?

Choose the section that tells the truth most clearly. You do not need a separate section for every type of learning.

Education

Use Education for degrees, diplomas, recognised academic programmes, and substantial formal study. A short online course normally does not belong beside a degree unless it is part of a larger programme or directly helps explain your preparation for the role.

Certifications

Use Certifications for a named credential that can be checked against an issuing body’s requirements. Include the credential name, issuer, completion or issue date, and expiry or renewal date when relevant. Do not turn a completion certificate into a licence or regulated qualification.

Professional Development

This is usually the clearest home for short online courses, workshops, seminars, and current learning. List only the most relevant items. A compact entry can include the course title, issuer, completion date, and one skill or assessment when that detail adds useful context.

Projects

Put a course project in Projects when the work itself demonstrates the skill. Name the project, explain your contribution, mention the tools or method, and state the result. The course can appear as a brief context line rather than the main achievement.

How to write a useful course or certificate entry

  1. Use the official name: copy the credential title accurately, without upgrading “introductory” to “advanced.”
  2. Show status: include “completed,” “in progress,” an issue date, or an expiry date where it matters.
  3. Add verification context: name the issuer or credential ID only if a reader could reasonably use it to verify the item. Never publish a private access code.
  4. Connect it to evidence: mention a project, assessment, tool, or work outcome that shows how you used the learning.
  5. Keep the list selective: prioritize items that match the role and are recent enough to represent your current knowledge.

Data visualisation with Python — Completed March 2026
Applied the course methods in a personal dashboard project using pandas and matplotlib.

Certifications

Three realistic resume examples

Recent graduate

PROFESSIONAL DEVELOPMENT
Research methods for social science — Completed 2026
Designed a small survey, cleaned 180 responses, and presented findings in a five-page report.

PROJECTS
Community survey analysis — University capstone
Built a coding guide, checked response quality, and summarized three practical recommendations for a local community group.

The course belongs in Professional Development because it records focused study. The project earns its own entry because it demonstrates research and analysis in action. For broader first-resume structure, see the student resume guide, but do not copy its student-specific sections if they do not fit your situation.

Career changer

PROFESSIONAL DEVELOPMENT
Foundations of service operations — Completed 2025
Topics included workflow mapping, service documentation, and basic performance reporting.

SELECTED PROJECT
Volunteer scheduling workflow — Community organisation
Mapped the intake process, created a shared schedule, and reduced missed handoffs across a six-person volunteer team.

The course supports the direction of the transition, but the project carries the proof. Keep previous employment in Experience and use this section to show how your new learning connects to a real responsibility.

Technical professional

CERTIFICATIONS
Cloud security fundamentals certificate — Issuing body, earned 2026
Credential ID available on request; no expiry stated.

PROFESSIONAL DEVELOPMENT
Infrastructure as code patterns — Completed 2025

A technical candidate can keep a verified credential prominent and move a shorter course into Professional Development. If a credential has renewal requirements, show its current status rather than relying on the title alone.

How to handle in-progress learning

In-progress learning can be useful when it is genuinely relevant and you state its status plainly. Use a future completion date only when you have a realistic plan. For example:

PROFESSIONAL DEVELOPMENT
Introduction to project scheduling — In progress, expected completion October 2026
Current work: building a sample delivery plan with dependencies, milestones, and a risk log.

Do not list an in-progress course under Certifications as if the credential already exists. If the role requires a licence or regulated qualification, explain your actual eligibility separately and follow the employer’s instructions.

What to do with expired certifications

An expired credential should not look current. Remove it when it no longer adds relevant evidence. If the history matters—for example, it explains work you completed during a previous period—label it clearly: “Credential name — expired 2024; held 2021–2024.” Never omit an expiry date when the reader needs it to judge current eligibility.

Do not do this

  • Do not inflate a course: a course completion is not a professional licence, degree, or regulated certification.
  • Do not hide dates: include completion, issue, renewal, or expiry information when status could be misunderstood.
  • Do not claim verification you do not have: never invent an issuer, credential ID, exam result, accreditation, or assessment.
  • Do not list every lesson: a long catalogue makes relevant evidence harder to find.
  • Do not use course names as proof of skill: pair important learning with a project, task, assessment, or result you can explain.
  • Do not overcrowd the resume: remove low-relevance or outdated learning before shrinking the type or margins.

A final check before you send the resume

  1. Can you explain exactly what was completed and who issued the record?
  2. Does every item have the right section and status?
  3. Are dates and current/expired labels clear?
  4. Is each item relevant to this role rather than merely interesting?
  5. Does at least one important course connect to a project or work example?
  6. Would a reader understand the entry without assuming a stronger credential?

Once the content is accurate, place it in a focused layout. Use the space saved by removing weak entries to make your strongest evidence easier to read.

Frequently asked questions

Should I list every online course on my resume?

No. Include only completed or current learning that is relevant to the target role and that you can describe accurately. A short, selective list is stronger than a catalogue.

Is a course certificate the same as a professional licence?

No. A course certificate records learning or completion, while a professional licence is an authorization governed by a regulator or professional body. Label each item accurately.

Where should I put an online course that I have not finished?

Use Professional Development or a similar section and mark it clearly as in progress, with an expected completion date if you have one. Do not call it completed or certified.

Can an online course project go in the Projects section?

Yes, when the project demonstrates a relevant skill. Name the course as context, then describe what you built, analyzed, or delivered rather than listing a lesson title.

Should I include an expired certification?

Usually not as a current credential. If the expired certification explains relevant past work, label it as expired and include the dates; never imply that it is active.
